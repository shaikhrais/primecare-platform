List of responsibilities for a Shift Supervisor:
1. Oversee daily operations and ensure staff adherence to protocols.
2. Manage shift schedules and allocate resources effectively.
3. Monitor caregiver performance and provide feedback.
4. Address any issues or conflicts that arise during the shift.
5. Ensure compliance with health and safety regulations.
6. Maintain communication with caregivers and clients.
7. Report on shift performance and operational metrics.
8. Conduct training and onboarding for new staff.
9. Handle emergencies and make quick decisions as needed.
10. Collaborate with other departments to improve service delivery.

Red flags of operation:
1. High number of unassigned shifts.
2. Caregivers reporting low battery or connectivity issues.
3. Frequent complaints from clients about service delays.
4. Caregivers showing signs of fatigue or burnout.
5. Inconsistent caregiver performance or attendance.
6. Lack of communication between caregivers and supervisors.
7. Unresolved conflicts among staff members.
8. Failure to meet compliance standards or regulations.
9. Delays in dispatching caregivers to clients.
10. High turnover rate among caregivers.

Dashboard needs for a Shift Supervisor:
1. Real-time overview of caregiver status (active, idle, traveling).
2. List of unassigned shifts with priority levels.
3. Map view showing caregiver locations and estimated arrival times.
4. Alerts for high-priority shifts or emergencies.
5. Performance metrics for caregivers (e.g., on-time arrivals, client feedback).
6. Communication tools for contacting caregivers and clients.
7. Shift schedule overview with coverage gaps highlighted.
8. Historical data on shift performance for trend analysis.
9. Compliance checklists and reminders.
10. Tools for reporting and documenting incidents or issues.
