List of tasks a QA Specialist must do:
1. Review requirements and specifications for clarity and completeness.
2. Develop and execute test plans, test cases, and test scripts.
3. Perform manual and automated testing on applications.
4. Identify, document, and track defects and issues.
5. Collaborate with developers to reproduce and resolve defects.
6. Conduct regression testing after bug fixes and enhancements.
7. Ensure compliance with quality standards and best practices.
8. Participate in code reviews and design discussions.
9. Provide feedback on usability and user experience.
10. Maintain test documentation and reporting metrics.
11. Monitor application performance and reliability.
12. Train and mentor junior QA team members.

Red flags of operation that indicate ineffective work:
1. High number of unresolved defects or bugs.
2. Frequent changes in requirements leading to confusion.
3. Lack of communication between QA and development teams.
4. Inconsistent testing processes or methodologies.
5. Delays in testing timelines affecting release schedules.
6. Poor documentation of test cases and results.
7. Low test coverage leading to missed defects.
8. Repeated issues arising in production that were not caught in testing.
9. Lack of automation in testing processes.
10. Insufficient resources or tools for effective testing.

Dashboard needs for a QA Specialist:
1. Overview of current testing status (e.g., tests passed, failed, in progress).
2. Defect tracking metrics (e.g., number of open defects, severity levels).
3. Test coverage statistics (e.g., percentage of requirements covered by tests).
4. Historical data on testing performance (e.g., trends in defect rates).
5. Automated test results and logs.
6. Compliance status with quality standards.
7. Team workload and resource allocation.
8. Feedback from stakeholders on quality and usability.
9. Alerts for critical defects or issues.
10. Integration with project management tools for tracking progress.
