List of tasks a QA Specialist must do:
1. Review and analyze requirements and specifications.
2. Develop and execute test plans and test cases.
3. Perform manual and automated testing.
4. Identify, document, and track defects.
5. Collaborate with development teams to resolve issues.
6. Conduct regression testing after fixes.
7. Ensure compliance with quality standards and best practices.
8. Participate in code reviews and design discussions.
9. Provide feedback on product usability and functionality.
10. Maintain test documentation and reports.

Red flags in operations that indicate ineffective work:
1. High number of defects reported post-release.
2. Frequent missed deadlines for testing phases.
3. Lack of communication between QA and development teams.
4. Inconsistent testing processes and methodologies.
5. Insufficient test coverage for critical features.
6. Repeated issues that were previously resolved.
7. Low team morale or high turnover rates in QA.
8. Inadequate documentation of test cases and results.
9. Delays in defect resolution and feedback loops.
10. Lack of automation in repetitive testing tasks.

Essential elements for a QA Specialist dashboard:
1. Test case execution status (pass/fail rates).
2. Defect tracking metrics (open, closed, and in-progress defects).
3. Test coverage percentage.
4. Regression testing results.
5. Performance metrics of automated tests.
6. Team workload and resource allocation.
7. Historical data on defect trends and patterns.
8. Compliance and standards adherence metrics.
9. Feedback from stakeholders on product quality.
10. Integration with project management tools for tracking progress.
