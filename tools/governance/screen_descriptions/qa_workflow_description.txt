List of tasks a QA Specialist must perform:

1. Review and analyze requirements and specifications.
2. Develop and execute test plans, test cases, and test scripts.
3. Perform manual and automated testing.
4. Identify, document, and track defects and issues.
5. Collaborate with development teams to resolve defects.
6. Conduct regression testing after fixes and enhancements.
7. Participate in code reviews and design discussions.
8. Ensure compliance with quality standards and best practices.
9. Provide feedback on usability and user experience.
10. Maintain test documentation and reports.
11. Monitor and report on testing progress and results.
12. Continuously improve testing processes and methodologies.

Red flags of operation that indicate ineffective work:

1. High number of defects found in production.
2. Frequent missed deadlines for testing phases.
3. Lack of communication between QA and development teams.
4. Incomplete or poorly written test cases.
5. Inconsistent testing results across different environments.
6. Low test coverage of critical features.
7. High turnover rate within the QA team.
8. Resistance to adopting new testing tools or methodologies.
9. Lack of clear requirements or specifications.
10. Insufficient time allocated for testing.

Dashboard needs for a QA Specialist:

1. Overview of current testing status (in progress, completed, blocked).
2. Metrics on defect density and severity.
3. Test coverage percentage for different modules.
4. Historical data on defect trends over time.
5. Summary of test case execution results (pass/fail).
6. Alerts for critical defects or issues.
7. Performance metrics for automated tests.
8. Integration with project management tools for tracking progress.
9. Visual representation of testing timelines and milestones.
10. Feedback and suggestions from team members on process improvements.
