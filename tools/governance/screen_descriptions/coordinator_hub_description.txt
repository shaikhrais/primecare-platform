List of responsibilities for a Shift Supervisor:
1. Monitor and manage the performance of caregivers.
2. Assign shifts to caregivers based on availability and qualifications.
3. Address and resolve alerts related to caregiver performance or client issues.
4. Ensure that all shifts are filled in a timely manner.
5. Maintain communication with caregivers and clients to ensure satisfaction.
6. Track and report on caregiver attendance and punctuality.
7. Analyze operational data to identify trends and areas for improvement.
8. Provide support and guidance to caregivers as needed.
9. Ensure compliance with company policies and regulations.
10. Conduct regular team meetings to discuss performance and operational updates.

Red flags of operation:
1. High number of unresolved alerts indicating potential issues with caregiver performance.
2. Frequent late arrivals or missed shifts by caregivers.
3. Low caregiver availability leading to unfilled shifts.
4. High turnover rate among caregivers.
5. Negative feedback from clients regarding caregiver performance.
6. Inconsistent communication between caregivers and the supervisory team.
7. Lack of timely updates on shift assignments and changes.
8. Increased number of incidents or complaints related to care provided.

Dashboard needs for a Shift Supervisor:
1. Overview of active caregivers with status updates.
2. List of open shifts with details on client, time, and required roles.
3. Summary of active alerts with severity levels.
4. Metrics on caregiver performance, including attendance and punctuality.
5. Notifications for unresolved alerts and high-priority shifts.
6. Visual representation of caregiver assignments and shift coverage.
7. Historical data on shift fulfillment and caregiver performance trends.
8. Quick access buttons for common actions (e.g., assigning shifts, resolving alerts).
9. Communication tools for direct messaging with caregivers.
10. Reporting tools for generating performance reports and operational insights.
